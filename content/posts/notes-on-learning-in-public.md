Most of what I know, I learned from someone else's half-finished notes. Not from
polished tutorials — from a rough page where somebody wrote down what confused
them and what finally made it click.

So this is where I keep mine.

## The useful part is the confusion

A finished explanation hides the path that produced it. It tells you the theorem
and not the three wrong approaches that came first. But the wrong approaches are
where the learning lives, and they are what a reader in the same position
actually needs.

When I write something up here, I try to keep:

- the question in the form I first asked it, before I knew better wording
- the approach that failed, and the specific reason it failed
- the smallest example that made the idea concrete

## Writing before you are ready

The obvious objection is that publishing unfinished work is publishing mistakes.
That is true, and it is the point. A mistake written down is a mistake that can
be corrected — by me later, or by someone kind enough to point it out.

The alternative is a folder of private notes that nobody reads, including me.

## How this works in practice

I write these in Markdown, commit them, and the site rebuilds itself. There is no
database and no admin panel, which means there is nothing to maintain and nothing
to break. The whole system is a folder of text files.

That is deliberate. The moment writing requires infrastructure, it stops
happening.
